WES Series 6 | Quality Control for Raw WES FASTQ Data

“Raw data are like freshly cooked rice: they may look steaming hot and ready, but their quality must still be checked before they can be made into excellent sushi.”

🎯 1. Why Perform Quality Control (QC)?

When we receive raw FASTQ files from a sequencing platform, we cannot proceed directly to variant analysis. The reason is simple: sequencing can introduce various forms of “contamination” or noise, including:

  • Low-quality bases: common near the ends of reads and detrimental to alignment accuracy.

  • Adapter-sequence contamination: adapters may be sequenced when the read length exceeds the insert size.

  • GC-content bias: extreme-GC regions can produce uneven coverage.

  • Duplicate sequences: potentially redundant data introduced by PCR amplification.

The purpose of QC is to identify and address these potential problems before analysis begins.

📂 2. Reviewing the FASTQ File Structure

Each sequencing read consists of four lines:

  1. An ID line beginning with @: contains the sample name, sequencing lane, and other information

  2. The nucleotide sequence: composed of A/T/C/G

  3. The + separator: may optionally be followed by the ID

  4. The quality-value line: contains the Phred quality score for each base

The relationship between a Phred quality score (Q score) and the sequencing error rate is:

  • Q20: error rate of approximately 1%

  • Q30: error rate of approximately 0.1%

WES data generally require ≥80% of bases to reach Q30. As sequencing technology has advanced, some platforms and projects have begun to use higher quality benchmarks, such as Q40 (an error rate of approximately 0.01%) or even Q50 (approximately 0.001%), in pursuit of greater sequencing accuracy and more reliable downstream analysis.

🛠 3. Common Quality-Control Metrics

Metric Description Reference standard or consideration
Base-quality distribution Examines the distribution of quality values at each position; low quality is common near read ends WES generally requires Q30≥80%
Sequence-length distribution Sequencing reads should have a consistent length An abnormal distribution may indicate a library or sequencing problem
GC-content distribution Should approximate the GC content of the human genome (approximately 38%–42%) Large deviations may indicate library bias or contamination; because sequencing platforms have GC-related preferences, the observed mean GC content may approach 50%
Adapter-contamination rate Adapter sequence may be read during sequencing Residual adapter sequence can cause alignment errors and should be removed before analysis
Sequence-duplication rate Proportion of reads with duplicate sequences A high duplication rate may reduce usable information and is often caused by excessive PCR amplification or excessive sequencing depth

This sequence-duplication rate is a FASTQ-level statistic and cannot be equated directly with positional duplicates in a BAM file. For the distinction between these definitions, see NGS Duplicates: What Exactly Is Being Duplicated?

🔍 4. Quality-Control and Processing Workflow

Many current analysis workflows use fastp directly. It combines quality control and read cleaning in a single tool, can replace combinations such as FastQC plus Cutadapt, is fast and resource-efficient, and automatically generates HTML and JSON reports containing quality distributions, GC content, adapter detection, and other information for rapid review.

fastp can automatically detect and remove adapters; perform per-position quality trimming, sliding-window trimming, and length/low-complexity filtering; process polyG/polyX sequences; correct overlaps and remove adapters from paired-end reads; estimate duplication; parse and record UMIs; and run in parallel across multiple threads.

Common command example (paired-end data):

fastp \
  -i sample_R1.fastq.gz -I sample_R2.fastq.gz \
  -o sample_R1.clean.fastq.gz -O sample_R2.clean.fastq.gz \
  --detect_adapter_for_pe \
  --cut_front --cut_tail --cut_window_size 4 --cut_mean_quality 20 \
  --length_required 50 \
  --thread 8 \
  --html sample_fastp.html --json sample_fastp.json

Parameters: --detect_adapter_for_pe automatically identifies paired-end adapters; --cut_* controls sliding-window quality trimming; --length_required sets the minimum retained length; --html/--json produces reports; and --thread specifies the number of threads.

💡 5. Tips for Interpreting QC Results

  • If Q scores are low overall: check library-preparation quality or sequencer status.

  • If the GC distribution is abnormal: consider contamination, such as bacterial DNA.

  • If the duplication rate is high: sequencing depth or PCR amplification may be excessive.

📌 Summary

Quality control is the first “security checkpoint” in WES analysis. It effectively reduces noise in downstream analyses and gives us greater confidence when interpreting the final results.
