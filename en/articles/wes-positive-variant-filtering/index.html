Criteria for Prioritizing Positive Variants in Whole-Exome Sequencing

I. Introduction

Whole-exome sequencing (WES), an efficient approach for detecting genetic variants, is widely used in oncology, rare inherited diseases, immune disorders, and other fields. By focusing on protein-coding regions (the exome), WES can capture most functionally relevant variants at a manageable cost. After variant calling, selecting biologically meaningful “positive variants” from the vast amount of variant data is a critical step in downstream analysis and interpretation.

This article systematically reviews the criteria for prioritizing positive variants after variant calling in WES data and, drawing on practical project experience, provides a highly actionable filtering framework.

II. Brief Overview of the WES Variant-Calling Workflow

  1. Sample preparation and library construction: DNA extraction → library construction → exome capture.
  2. Sequencing platform: Illumina NovaSeq/HiSeq, etc.
  3. Data processing:
    • Quality control (FastQC, Trimmomatic)
    • Alignment (BWA-MEM to hg38 or hg19)
    • Deduplication (Picard)
    • Variant calling (GATK HaplotypeCaller / Mutect2)
  4. Variant annotation:
    • Tools: ANNOVAR, VEP, SnpEff
    • Databases: dbSNP, ClinVar, 1000G, gnomAD, COSMIC, etc.

III. Criteria for Prioritizing Positive Variants

The goals of prioritizing positive variants are to improve confidence, exclude sequencing false positives, and focus on potentially pathogenic or functional variants. The specific criteria are as follows:

1. Sequencing Quality Filters

Metric Filtering Criterion Description
Mapping Quality (MQ) MQ ≥ 30 Exclude low-quality alignments
Depth (DP) DP ≥ 10 (somatic) or ≥20 (germline) Ensure adequate coverage
Variant Quality (QUAL) QUAL ≥ 100 Reflect variant-call confidence
Variant Allele Frequency (VAF) Generally ≥ 5% (somatic) or 40–60% (heterozygous germline) Set the lower limit for variant abundance
Strand Bias (FS, SOR) FS ≤ 60, SOR ≤ 3 Exclude variants with marked strand bias

2. Database Evidence and Population-Frequency Filters

  • Population-frequency filters:

    • Frequency < 0.01 (1%) in gnomAD/ExAC/1000 Genomes
    • For rare-disease analysis, a frequency < 0.001 (0.1%) is generally required

  • Known pathogenicity databases:

    • ClinVar: select variants classified as “Pathogenic” or “Likely pathogenic”
    • COSMIC: used to assess tumor-associated variants

3. Biological-Function Annotation

Retain only variants that may alter protein function:

Variant Type Retain? Description
Nonsense variant Yes Introduces a premature stop codon
Missense variant Yes Causes an amino acid change; functional prediction is also required
Splice-site variant Yes May affect mRNA splicing
Frameshift variant Yes Highly likely to be pathogenic
Synonymous variant No Generally nonpathogenic unless it is a known functional variant

Functional prediction tools such as SIFT, PolyPhen-2, and CADD can be used to assess the potential impact of missense variants.

4. Somatic Variant-Specific Filtering (for Tumors)

  • Filter benign variants: exclude common SNPs (variants with an rs identifier in dbSNP plus a high population frequency)
  • Comparison against matched normal: when a matched normal sample is available, retain only tumor-specific variants
  • VAF ranges:
    • Clonal variants: VAF > 20%
    • Subclonal variants: VAF 5%–20%
  • Remove FFPE artifacts: assess C>T / G>A enrichment in conjunction with the output of Mutect2 FilterMutectCalls

5. Technical Replication and Validation Recommendations

  • Cross-validation:
    • Use the intersection of results from different callers (e.g., Mutect2 and VarScan2)
    • Confirm on another platform (e.g., validation with a panel)
  • Biological validation:
    • Sanger sequencing
    • qPCR/ddPCR validation of variant abundance
    • RNA-seq validation of effects at the expression level

IV. Common Considerations

  1. Reference-genome version consistency: annotation and alignment must use the same build (hg19 vs. hg38)
  2. FFPE-specific noise: watch for C>T / G>A artifacts
  3. Batch effects and contamination monitoring: add multisample QC (e.g., VerifyBamID, ContEst)
  4. Combine automation with review: a standardized Snakemake or Nextflow workflow is recommended, but important variants should be reviewed manually (e.g., in IGV)
