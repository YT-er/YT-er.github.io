FFPE RNA-seq: A Guide to Pre-Sequencing Quality Control

At a Glance

  • Primary metric: DV200. DV200 >70%: high quality; 50–70%: moderate; 30–50%: low quality; <30%: library preparation is not recommended.

  • RIN is not sensitive for FFPE samples and is commonly 2–3; it is insufficient for predicting whether library preparation will succeed. Base decisions on DV200 and concentration.

  • Recommended input (total RNA): 20 ng when DV200 >70%; 20–40 ng when DV200 is 50–70%; 50-100 ng when DV200 is 30–50%.

  • Priority of library-preparation strategies: rRNA depletion ≥ capture enrichment (RNA exome/targeted exons) ≫ poly(A)-only enrichment (FFPE samples are prone to 3′-end bias).

Why Is FFPE Challenging?

FFPE (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded) preservation provides excellent tissue morphology but also causes nucleic-acid crosslinking, fragmentation, and chemical modification. The direct consequences are:

  1. RNA length is markedly reduced, decreasing the proportion of usable fragments >200 nt;

  2. RIN loses discriminatory power, with atypical electropherogram profiles and poorly defined rRNA peaks;

  3. Reverse-transcription and PCR efficiency decrease, potentially introducing base errors;

  4. Library complexity is low and the duplication rate is high, requiring greater sequencing depth to achieve comparable sensitivity. The goal of pre-sequencing quality control is therefore to identify as many “usable” samples as possible and match low-quality samples with more robust library-preparation and sequencing parameters.

Key Quality-Control Metrics and Interpretation

1) DV200 (Proportion of Fragments >200 nt)

Definition: the percentage of RNA fragments in the sample that are longer than 200 nt. A higher DV200 indicates that more usable fragments are available for library preparation. Recommended thresholds:

  • >70% (high): fragments are relatively long, and the success rate of library preparation and stability of expression quantification are high. A 20 ng input is recommended.

  • 50–70% (moderate): usable, but the input should be increased appropriately to 20–40 ng; during library preparation, monitor short-fragment enrichment and 3′ bias.

  • 30–50% (low): highly degraded. An input of 40-100 ng is recommended; prioritize rRNA depletion or capture-based methods and apply stringent release criteria.

  • <30% (very low): library preparation is not recommended. If the sample is extremely precious, a capture-based, low-input strategy may be attempted, but the boundary of “research use only/not reportable” must be explicitly defined in the SOP.

2) RIN

Values of 2–3 are common in FFPE samples, and RIN does not have discriminatory power. It may be recorded but should not be used as a release criterion.

3) Concentration and Purity

  • Quantification: prioritize Qubit/fluorometric quantification (well suited to short fragments and avoids overestimation by NanoDrop).

  • Purity: A260/280 ≈ 2.0; A260/230 should preferably be >1.8. Contaminants such as protein, salts, phenol, or residual xylene inhibit reverse transcription and PCR.

  • DNA contamination: DNase treatment is essential during extraction; include a no-RT control for verification when necessary.

4) Histology and Sample Information

  • Age of the paraffin block: preferably ≤3 years; degradation increases with prolonged storage.

  • Tumor content: preferably ≥20–30%; enrich the tumor area by macrodissection to improve the signal-to-noise ratio.

  • Sample amount and area: record section thickness and number of sections to support batch comparability and traceability.

Key Procedures Before and After Extraction

Before Extraction

  • Select unstained sections; avoid residual H&E stain, which may interfere with purification.

  • Perform thorough deparaffinization (xylene/substitute) → sequential graded ethanol washes to remove solvent → complete drying; residual organic solvent inhibits downstream reactions.

  • Estimate tissue amount and tumor content, and create sample records and chain-of-custody records in the LIMS.

Recommended Extraction Procedure

  • Perform thorough proteinase K digestion (which may be extended to several hours);

  • Include a high-temperature crosslink-reversal step (e.g., staged treatment at 65–80 °C) to improve reversal of crosslinks;

  • Use DNase digestion to remove genomic DNA;

  • Purify using a silica column or magnetic beads; whenever possible, choose a commercial kit designed for FFPE material;

  • Follow strict RNase-free procedures and minimize freeze–thaw cycles.

Rapid Post-Extraction QC (Release Criteria)

  1. Qubit concentration ≥ the specified minimum input (stratified by DV200);

  2. DV200 meets the project threshold (≥30% or a more stringent team standard);

  3. A260/280 and A260/230 fall within acceptable ranges;

  4. For low-volume samples, a subset may undergo Bioanalyzer/TapeStation analysis to confirm fragment-size distribution (optional).

Recommendation: Link DV200 and minimum input as a “binary release criterion.” For example: DV200 ≥50% and input ≥20 ng; DV200 30–50% and input ≥40 ng. Samples that do not meet the criteria are designated QC Fail (with optional review/re-extraction procedures).

Library-Preparation Strategies and Sequencing Parameters

1) Prioritize rRNA Depletion

  • Rationale: damage to poly(A) tails is common in FFPE samples, and poly(A)-only enrichment readily causes 3′ bias and information loss.

  • Advantages: more tolerant of fragmented RNA; covers mRNA and some noncoding RNA, providing a more comprehensive expression profile.

  • Considerations: background complexity increases; library size and adapter dimers must be controlled.

2) Capture Enrichment (RNA Exome/Targeted Exons/Targeted Panel)

  • Advantages: well suited to short fragments; even scattered fragments can still be captured by probes as long as they fall within target regions;

  • Applications: translational-research/clinical panels (expression quantification, fusion detection, alternative-splicing hotspots, etc.);

  • Low input: some methods support stable library preparation from 10–50 ng, substantially expanding the range of usable samples;

  • UMIs: if low-abundance fusions/variants are of interest, prioritize methods that include UMIs.

3) Trade-Offs of Poly(A) Enrichment

  • Advantages: low background and a focus on mature mRNA;

  • Disadvantages: sensitive to poly(A)-tail integrity in FFPE samples, with pronounced 3′-end bias;

  • Recommendation: consider only when DV200 is relatively high (≥70%) and the objective is conventional expression profiling.

4) Recommended Technical Parameters

  • Fragmentation: most FFPE samples do not require additional fragmentation because the RNA is already naturally short;

  • Insert size: keep within the ~200–300 bp range to prevent adapter read-through caused by inserts that are too short;

  • Number of PCR cycles: FFPE samples often require 1–3 additional cycles to achieve an acceptable library yield, but overamplification must be avoided;

  • Sequencing read length: PE75–PE100 is recommended; longer reads provide limited benefit for extremely short fragments;

  • Sequencing depth: for whole-transcriptome sequencing, ≥40–50M read pairs/sample is recommended; for targeted panels, follow the manufacturer's or empirically established coverage thresholds (e.g., gene-level coverage ≥500–1000×).

Effects of FFPE on Downstream Data and Mitigation Strategies

  • Higher duplication rate and lower complexity: increase sequencing depth appropriately while applying deduplication/UMI quantification during bioinformatic analysis;

  • 3′-end bias: prioritize rRNA-depletion or capture workflows; use gene-body models/segment-level normalization for expression quantification;

  • Increased alignment mismatches: modestly relax alignment parameters and strengthen soft-clipping and mismatch filters;

  • Fusion-detection sensitivity: increase depth and the minimum supporting-read threshold, and use a capture/AMP strategy;

  • Batch effects: strengthen the control system (matched FF/FFPE samples, reference RNA/ERCC) and use batch correction (e.g., ComBat) to support assessment.

Release Decision

  1. Sample registration → histological assessment (tumor content ≥20–30%? block age ≤3 years?) → record whether macrodissection was performed;

  2. Extraction (deparaffinization → digestion → crosslink reversal → DNase → purification) → Qubit quantification and purity assessment;

  3. DV200 measurement:

  • ≥70% → Release category A: rRNA depletion, capture, or poly(A) can be used; input 20 ng;

  • 50–70% → Release category B: prioritize rRNA depletion or capture; input 40–50 ng;

  • 30–50% → Release category C: rRNA depletion or capture only; input 50–100 ng; use UMIs if necessary;

  • <30% → Do not release/research-use pathway (requires supervisor approval and disclosure of risk).

  1. Library QC: library fragment peak at 200–300 bp, adapter dimers <2%, and acceptable quantification;

  2. Sequencing-run QC: Q30, duplication rate, on-target rate, and depth meet expected thresholds;

  3. Data QC: number of detected genes, inter-sample correlation, and positive controls for fusions/variants pass → report.

Common Pitfalls and Troubleshooting

  • Residual xylene/solvent: wash thoroughly with ethanol and dry completely;

  • Genomic DNA contamination: strengthen the DNase step and include a no-RT control;

  • Insufficient tissue: combine multiple sections and increase the extraction volume;

  • Excessive PCR: if the library is too short and displays a step-like peak profile, optimize the cycle number and input;

  • Extreme 3′ bias: switch to rRNA depletion or capture;

  • Excessive duplication rate: use a UMI strategy, increase total sequencing depth, and optimize input and fragment-size distribution;

  • Large batch-to-batch differences: use fixed controls, standardize the SOP, strengthen trend-chart monitoring, and implement corrective and preventive action (CAPA).

Quality-Control Thresholds

  • Release criteria: DV200 ≥30% (may be raised to ≥40–50%); Qubit ≥ minimum input; A260/280 ≈ 2.0.

  • Recommended input:

  • DV200 ≥70%: 20 ng;

  • 50–70%: 20–40 ng;

  • 30–50%: 40-100 ng.

  • Library targets: insert size 200–300 bp; adapter dimers <2%; number of PCR cycles per the manufacturer's recommendation +1–3 (without overamplification).

  • Sequencing recommendations: PE75–PE100; whole transcriptome ≥40–50M read pairs; for panels, follow the on-target coverage threshold.

  • Data release: duplication rate, on-target rate, and number of detected genes are consistent with batch controls; positive controls meet acceptance criteria.

Summary

FFPE samples are not inherently incompatible with RNA-seq. With an FFPE-oriented extraction strategy, DV200-centered release logic, and a library-preparation method matched to the characteristics of the material, supported by clinical-grade SOPs and end-to-end traceability, the great majority of FFPE samples can produce data that are interpretable, reproducible, and reportable. Once these practices become routine in the laboratory, FFPE will shift from being a “difficult sample” to a “controllable sample.”
